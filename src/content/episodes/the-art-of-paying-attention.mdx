## The conversation

What changes when we stop looking for the next big thing and start noticing what is already here? A conversation about the quiet practice of paying attention.

Some of the most useful conversations leave you with something small: a question, a practice, or a new way of seeing the familiar. This episode is an invitation to slow down and make a little space for that.

### A few things to take with you

- The ordinary as a starting point.
- What we miss when we rush.
- A small practice for tomorrow.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try this after listening">
  Take ten minutes away from your usual routine. Notice one thing you would
  normally pass by, and write a sentence about it.
</Callout>

## Transcript

**Alex:** Hey, welcome to Iris. I'm Alex. I took the long way home yesterday, and noticed a little garden behind a wall. I've walked past that wall for years. Never saw the garden. It's funny how much is there when you're not in a hurry to get somewhere else.

**Alex:** That's where this conversation with Elliot Reed begins. He's a writer who pays attention to the things most of us walk straight past. And the more we talked, the more I realized that noticing isn't really a special talent. It's making a bit of room. Putting your phone away. Letting someone finish their thought before you reach for your own.

**Alex:** So, here's a small thing to try. On your next walk, take a different turn. Look for something you haven't seen before, even if it's just the color of a door.
