There is a moment before every recording when the questions on my desk stop looking like a plan and start looking like possibilities.

I do prepare. I read, listen, and write down the things I’m curious about. But I’ve learned that a good conversation needs space to move away from that preparation.

## Questions, not a checklist

Instead of arranging questions into a fixed order, I try to find a few places we might begin. A recent project. A change of mind. A detail that seems small but feels important.

The difference is subtle. A checklist asks whether we covered everything. Curiosity asks whether we found something worth spending time with.

## Leave a little silence

Silence can feel uncomfortable on a microphone. My instinct is to fill it. But often, the next thought arrives just after the moment I would have interrupted.

I’m practicing waiting one beat longer. Not for drama. Just to give someone a chance to finish thinking.

## Follow what feels alive

When a guest lights up talking about something I didn’t expect, that is usually the place to go. The notes can wait. The point of being prepared is to feel comfortable enough to be surprised.

I think that is true of more than recording a podcast. A little preparation, a little attention, and enough room for the conversation to become itself.
