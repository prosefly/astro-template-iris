I started carrying a notebook because I kept forgetting the small things. Not appointments or shopping lists, but the sentence someone said on a train. The way the afternoon light fell across a kitchen table. A question that appeared in the middle of a walk.

At first, I tried to make the entries useful. There should be an idea here, I thought. Something I could turn into a piece of work. But that pressure made the notebook feel like another task.

## A place to pay attention

So I made a simpler rule: write down one thing I noticed. No lesson required.

On Monday, it was a hand-painted sign outside a bakery. On Tuesday, a friend’s laugh. On Wednesday, the fact that I had walked the same street for years without seeing the little garden behind a wall.

> You don’t have to turn everything you notice into something. Sometimes noticing is the whole thing.

The notebook became less of an archive and more of a way to practice looking. I found that once I had somewhere to put the small things, I started finding more of them.

## Try it for a week

1. Find something small enough to carry.
2. Write one sentence each day.
3. Resist the urge to make it profound.
4. At the end of the week, read the sentences together.

You might see a pattern. You might not. Either way, you will have spent a week paying a little more attention.

That feels like a good place to start.
